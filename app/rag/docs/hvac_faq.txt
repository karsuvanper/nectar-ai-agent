SECTION 1: HVAC OPERATING PROCEDURES

1. STANDARD START SEQUENCE:
   - Verify all safety interlocks are closed and LOTO tags are removed.
   - Ensure thermostat is in "AUTO" mode with appropriate temperature setpoint.
   - Confirm outdoor air dampers are in the minimum position.
   - Start chiller or boiler according to manufacturer's sequence of operation.
   - Monitor equipment gauges and amperage during ramp-up.
   - Allow system to stabilize before adjusting setpoints.

2. STANDARD STOP SEQUENCE:
   - Gradually reduce load before shutting down equipment.
   - Stop cooling/heating source first, then circulation pumps.
   - Verify equipment has come to complete stop before securing power.
   - Record run-time hours and any abnormal observations in logbook.

3. SEASONAL MODE TRANSITIONS:
   - Spring Transition: Switch from heating to cooling mode. Change chilled water setpoint from 45°F to 44.6°F. Verify changeover valves shift correctly. Remove any heating system interlocks.
   - Fall Transition: Switch from cooling to heating mode. Change chilled water setpoint to maintain space temperature above 70°F. Activate heating systems and verify proper operation before reducing cooling capacity.

4. NORMAL OPERATION MONITORING:
   - Check temperature differentials across heat exchangers weekly.
   - Monitor pressure readings against design specifications.
   - Review energy consumption trends for anomaly detection.
   - Ensure all control system setpoints are within approved ranges.

SECTION 3: AHU TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDES

AHU LOW AIRFLOW TROUBLESHOOTING:
1. Filter Condition:
   - Check filter differential pressure gauge.
   - Replace filters when differential pressure exceeds 0.5 inches WG (125 Pa).
   - Recommended replacement interval: every 3 months or per facility policy.

2. Belt Tension:
   - Inspect V-belts for cracks, fraying, or glazing.
   - Measure belt deflection: should deflect 1/2 inch (12.7 mm) under 5 lb (2.2 kg) force.
   - Adjust tension using motor base sliders to manufacturer specifications.
   - Replace belts if tension cannot be maintained within ±10% of spec.

3. Filter Replacement Differential Pressure Limits:
   - New filter initial pressure drop: ≤ 0.1 inches WG (25 Pa)
   - Filter replacement threshold: ≥ 0.5 inches WG (125 Pa)
   - High-efficiency filters: replacement at ≥ 1.0 inches WG (250 Pa)
   - Document pressure drop before and after replacement.

4. Coil Condition:
   - Inspect evaporator and condenser coils monthly.
   - Clean coils when dust accumulation exceeds 1/16 inch (1.6 mm).
   - Use compressed air or mild detergent solution for cleaning.
   - Verify coil fin alignment after cleaning.

5. Fan Motor and VFD:
   - Check motor amperage against nameplate rating.
   - VFD fault codes: refer to manufacturer troubleshooting guide.
   - Verify VFD programming matches motor nameplate RPM and voltage.

SECTION 4: MAINTENANCE PROCEDURES

PREVENTATIVE MAINTENANCE SCHEDULES:

1. DAILY CHECKS:
   - Visual equipment inspection for leaks, unusual noises, or vibrations.
   - Check pressure and temperature gauge readings.
   - Verify control system operation and alarm status.
   - Log run-time hours and any abnormal conditions.

2. WEEKLY CHECKS:
   - Inspect filter condition and record differential pressure.
   - Check belt tension on all belt-driven equipment.
   - Verify VFD operation and parameter settings.
   - Test emergency stop functionality.

3. MONTHLY COIL CLEANING:
   - Inspect evaporator and condenser coils.
   - Clean coils when dust accumulation exceeds 1/16 inch (1.6 mm).
   - Use compressed air (max 30 psi) or mild detergent solution.
   - Rinse thoroughly and allow to dry before restart.
   - Verify coil fin straightening if damaged.

4. BEARING LUBRICATION:
   - Lubricate bearings per manufacturer schedule (typically every 2,000-5,000 operating hours).
   - Use specified grease type and grade (e.g., Mobil Polyrex EM for chiller bearings).
   - Avoid over-lubrication: fill bearing to 1/3 to 1/2 of cavity space.
   - Record lubrication date, grease type, and amount applied.
   - Replace bearings if overheating, excessive noise, or vibration persists.

5. QUARTERLY INSPECTIONS:
   - Check belt alignment and condition.
   - Inspect fan blades for damage or imbalance.
   - Verify damper actuation and linkages.
   - Test safety controls and interlocks.

6. SEMI-ANNUAL MAINTENANCE:
   - Comprehensive chiller system inspection.
   - Refrigerant level check and leak detection.
   - Oil analysis and sampling (if applicable).
   - Heat exchanger performance testing.

7. ANNUAL MAINTENANCE:
   - Complete system overhaul per manufacturer recommendations.
   - Calibrate all control sensors and instruments.
   - Review and update maintenance records.
   - Plan upcoming year's maintenance schedule.

SECTION 5: SAFETY INSTRUCTIONS

LOCKOUT/TAGOUT (LOTO) PROCEDURES:

1. PREPARATION:
   - Identify all energy sources: electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, thermal, chemical.
   - Review equipment-specific LOTO procedures in maintenance manuals.
   - Notify affected personnel of planned shutdown.

2. SHUTDOWN:
   - Sequence: Remove load, then stop equipment per normal stop sequence.
   - Verify equipment has come to complete stop.

3. ISOLATION:
   - Identify and operate all energy isolation devices:
     * Electrical: Circuit breakers, disconnect switches, control transformers.
     * Mechanical: Valves, dampers, belt tension release.
     * Hydraulic/Pneumatic: Pressure relief, valve closure.
     * Thermal: Steam valve closure, fuel supply shutdown.
   - Lock out each isolation device with individual personal lock.

4. APPLICATION OF TAGS:
   - Tag must include: employee name, reason for lockout, date/time, contact information.
   - "DO NOT OPERATE" warning visible on each lock/tag.
   - Never remove another person's lock or tag.

5. ENERGY VERIFICATION:
   - Attempt to start equipment to confirm isolation (with emergency stop ready).
   - Verify zero energy state using appropriate testing equipment.
   - Measure voltage/power confirmed de-energized before work begins.

6. RE-ENERGIZATION:
   - Verify all tools and personnel are clear of equipment.
   - Remove locks and tags in reverse order of application.
   - Notify affected personnel before restart.
   - Monitor equipment during initial start-up.

REFRIGERANT LEAK SAFETY PROTOCOLS:

1. Detection:
   - Electronic leak detector: sensitivity ≤ 0.1 oz/year (2.8 g/year).
   - Soap solution test at joints and connections.
   - Olfactory detection (only if refrigerant has distinctive odor).

2. Immediate Actions:
   - Evacuate area of unauthorized personnel.
   - Increase ventilation to dilute refrigerant concentration.
   - Monitor air quality: ASHRAE Standard 34 concentration limits.
   - Post "Refrigerant Leak" warning signs at area entrance.

3. Repair Procedures:
   - Only certified EPA Section 608 technicians may handle refrigerants.
   - Recover refrigerant using approved recovery machine.
   - Repair leak per manufacturer specifications.
   - Evacuate system to required vacuum level (500 microns Hg).
   - Charge with correct refrigerant type and amount.
   - Leak test repaired area before putting back into service.

4. Reporting:
   - Record leak quantity, location, and repair actions in logbook.
   - Report leaks exceeding 15% of system charge annually to EPA.
   - Maintain records for minimum 3 years per EPA requirements.

EMERGENCY SHUTDOWNS:

1. TRIGGER CONDITIONS:
   - Fire or smoke near HVAC equipment.
   - Refrigerant leak with high concentration.
   - Electrical hazard or arc flash risk.
   - Severe equipment vibration or unusual noise.
   - Loss of control signals or safety interlocks.

2. PROCEDURE:
   - Activate emergency stop (E-stop) at local and central panels.
   - Isolate all energy sources per LOTO procedures (if maintenance required).
   - Evacuate affected area if necessary.
   - Contact emergency services (fire department, hazmat) for refrigerant leaks or electrical fires.
   - Document emergency event: time, conditions, actions taken, personnel involved.

3. POST-EMERGENCY:
   - Comprehensive equipment inspection before restart.
   - Test all safety systems and controls.
   - Review emergency response and update procedures if needed.
   - Notify facility manager and safety officer.

SECTION 8: TROUBLESHOOTING FAQS

COMMON ISSUES FAQ:

Q: Why is the chiller not cooling below 50°F?
A: Check chilled water setpoint, verify flow switch is closed, inspect control valve operation, ensure condenser water temperature is within design range (not exceeding 32°C/89.6°F). Low approach temperature may indicate restricted condenser water flow or fouled condenser coils.

Q: AHU is short-cycling. What should I check?
A: Check thermostat placement (away from heat sources), inspect control voltage wiring for interference, verify VFD programming, check filter differential pressure, ensure return air grille is unobstructed.

Q: Water leaking from chiller basepan.
A: Inspect condensate drain line for blockage, check condensate pump operation, verify drain pan is not overflowing, check for refrigerant line sweating (insulate suction line).

Q: High energy consumption on AHU-02.
A: Check if VFD is operating at partial speed unnecessarily, inspect filter condition (may need replacement), verify thermostat scheduling, look for duct leakage, check reheat coil operation.

Q: Chiller trips on high discharge temperature.
A: Verify condenser water flow is adequate, check condenser outlet temperature, inspect for non-condensables in refrigerant system, check oil level and condition, ensure cooling tower is operating properly.

Q: AHU circuit breaker keeps tripping.
A: Check motor amperage against nameplate (may indicate bearing failure or unbalanced voltage), inspect control circuit for shorts, verify VFD input voltage, check for restricted airflow (dirty filters/coils).

NOISE DIAGNOSTIC FAQ:

1. Rattling noise:
   - Likely cause: Loose panels, access doors, or ductwork.
   - Solution: Tighten all fasteners, secure access panels, check ductwork supports.

2. Screeching or squealing:
   - Likely cause: Worn fan belt, improper belt tension, failing motor bearings.
   - Solution: Replace belt, adjust tension, lubricate or replace motor bearings.

3. Banging or knocking:
   - Likely cause: Water hammer in piping, loose piping supports, expanding/contracting ducts.
   - Solution: Secure piping supports, install expansion joints, check for proper valve operation.

4. Low-frequency humming:
   - Likely cause: Motor operating outside design parameters, VFD carrier frequency issue, loose laminations.
   - Solution: Verify motor load, adjust VFD parameters, tighten motor mounting bolts.

SENSOR CALIBRATION GUIDES:

1. Temperature Sensor (RTD/thermistor):
   - Calibration reference: Ice point (0°C/32°F) and steam point (100°C/212°F).
   - Frequency: Annually or per facility calibration policy.
   - Procedure: Immerse sensor in controlled temperature baths, compare reading to reference thermometer, adjust DCS/SCADA if error > 0.5°F.

2. Pressure Sensor (differential pressure transmitter):
   - Calibration reference: Zero (no flow) and known reference pressure.
   - Frequency: Annually.
   - Procedure: Apply zero reference (vent to atmosphere), apply 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% of span, compare output (4-20 mA), adjust if error > 1% of span.

3. Flow Meter (turbine or ultrasonic):
   - Calibration reference: Reference flow measurement using calibrated meter.
   - Frequency: Annually for turbine meters, biennial for ultrasonic.
   - Procedure: Run test flow at multiple rates (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%), compare readings, calculate K-factor adjustment, apply to DCS.

4. CO2 Sensor (IAQ control):
   - Calibration reference: Fresh air (400 ppm) and calibrated span gas (typically 1000-2000 ppm).
   - Frequency: Semi-annually.
   - Procedure: Expose sensor to fresh air, zero adjustment, then expose to span gas, adjust reading to match certified gas concentration.
